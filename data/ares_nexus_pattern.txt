## **ABSTRACT** 

The rapid transition toward real-time cross-border settlement under ISO 20022, together with the stringent requirements of FINMA Guidance 08/2024 (18 December 2024), has exposed a critical “Trust Gap” in Tier-1 financial infrastructure: the inherent conflict between nondeterministic AI reasoning and the immutable consistency required by regulated financial ledgers. 

This paper introduces **Ares-Nexus** , a high-throughput settlement engine that implements the **Non-Executive AI (NEAI)** architectural pattern. By strictly decoupling probabilistic AI advisory signals from a deterministic Event-Sourced core, Ares-Nexus achieves a sustained core throughput of 10,000 TPS. The deterministic settlement path (Command →Event →Outbox) maintains a **P50 latency of 0.85 ms and P99 < 10 ms** . When the Non-Executive AI (NEAI) advisory layer is engaged, the end-to-end **asynchronous P99 latency remains < 50 ms** , ensuring that probabilistic reasoning overhead never bottlenecks the atomic financial core. 

Validated against multi-billion-dollar structured finance portfolios (ABS/RMBS) equivalent to those managed by the co-author at **Tier-1 global financial institutions** , the architecture significantly reduces reconciliation latency and prevents Risk-Weighted Asset (RWA) inflation under Basel IV. It provides a reproducible blueprint for integrating AI into mission-critical financial cores while maintaining full compliance with DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act), Swiss Bank Secrecy, and human accountability.

##### **ARES-NEXUS VS. CBDC Y LGO (LEDGER-ONLY ARCHITECTURES)** 

Unlike **Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC)** or **Local Government Outcomes (LGO)** experiments, which focus on asset tokenization and transaction finality in distributed networks, Ares-Nexus operates one layer higher: **Risk Governance** . While a **CBDC** guarantees that the token moves from point A to point B, Ares-Nexus guarantees that the movement is normatively valid under Basel IV before the ledger even notices. Current CBDCs lack built-in non-executive AI, delegating business logic to smart contracts that are often deterministic but rigid, or to external oracles that introduce latency and trust risks. 

##### **THE FALLACY OF ATOMIZATION IN KAFKA-BASED MICROSERVICES** 

Current systems using Kafka for AI signal propagation fail at Decision Atomicity. In a typical Kafka architecture, the "Settlement" event is published and AI agents consume it reactively. This creates a "Reconciliation Gap": the ledger has already changed state before the AI detects a Risk-Weighted Asset (RWA) risk. 

Ares-Nexus addresses this using the Transactional Outbox Pattern on PostgreSQL/Marten: the AI signal is an optional precondition but is evaluated before the event is persisted. In Kafka, if the AI consumer fails after the producer publishes the event, the system remains in a state of regulatory inconsistency. In Ares-Nexus, if there is no Decision Gate validation, there is no event on disk.

## **THE FINANCIAL DETERMINISM PRINCIPLE (FDP)** 

In regulated financial infrastructures, all state transitions affecting the financial ledger must be strictly deterministic, reproducible, and auditable. Any probabilistic system—including machine learning models, generative AI systems, or heuristic agents—must not directly execute financial state transitions. Instead, such systems may only produce advisory signals which are evaluated by a deterministic governance layer prior to execution. 

This architectural constraint ensures that financial state evolution remains replayable, regulatorauditable, and immune to non-deterministic inference behaviour. The Ares-Nexus architecture operationalizes this principle by enforcing a strict separation between AI advisory systems and deterministic settlement logic through the Non-Executive AI (NEAI) pattern. 

The Ares-Nexus framework is governed by the **Financial Determinism Principle** , which establishes that for any asynchronous AI-driven credit signal ( 𝑺 ), there exists a unique, immutable mapping to a ledger state ( 𝑳 ) such that: 

𝓜 𝑺,𝑪 →𝑳 **deterministic** 

Where 𝓜 represents the **Deterministic Mapping Function** , 𝑺 denotes the **Asynchronous AI-driven Signal** (probabilistic input), and 𝑪 represents the **Regulatory Invariant Constraints** (Basel IV/FINMA). Unlike legacy implementations where decision-making is coupled and probabilistic, FDT proves that by isolating 𝑺 through a non-executive boundary, the resulting ledger state 𝑳 remains immutable and verifiable. 

## **ARCHITECTURE AXIOMS** 

The Ares-Nexus architecture is based on a set of fundamental axioms that define the constraints under which financial systems can safely integrate artificial intelligence while preserving deterministic ledger behavior. 

These axioms represent architectural invariants that must hold for the system to remain compliant with regulatory and operational requirements. 

### **AXIOM 1 — LEDGER DETERMINISM** 

All financial state transitions must be deterministic functions of the system's event history. 

Formally, given an initial ledger state 𝒔𝟎 and an ordered sequence of events 𝑬 = {𝒆𝟏, 𝒆𝟐, ..., 𝒆𝒏} , the resulting state 𝒔𝒏 must be uniquely determined by the deterministic application of each event transition. 

This axiom ensures that financial state can always be reconstructed through event replay, enabling regulatory auditability and reconciliation across distributed systems. 

### **AXIOM 2 — AI NON-EXECUTABILITY** 

Artificial intelligence components must not directly execute state-changing financial operations. 

AI systems may observe system state and generate recommendations, but all state transitions must be executed exclusively by deterministic policy evaluation layers. 

This constraint ensures that probabilistic inference cannot directly influence financial ledger mutations, thereby preserving deterministic behavior in regulated financial environments. 

### **AXIOM 3 — EVENT IMMUTABILITY** 

All financial events recorded in the system's event log must be immutable. 

Once an event has been committed to the event store, it cannot be modified or deleted. Any corrections or adjustments must be represented as new events appended to the event stream. 

This property guarantees that the complete history of financial state transitions remains permanently auditable. 

Together, these axioms establish the architectural foundation of the Ares-Nexus system, ensuring that probabilistic AI reasoning can coexist with deterministic financial infrastructure without violating regulatory requirements for traceability, auditability, and reproducibility.

### **THE 95 % THRESHOLD AND META-EVALUATION OF HALLUCINATIONS** 

The confidence threshold ( 𝑺 **conf** ≥ 𝟎.𝟗𝟓 ) is not a static value of the model, but rather the result of a Verification Ensemble. To prevent an AI from becoming overconfident, Ares-Nexus implements a Meta-Evaluation Layer: 

1. **Logit-Distance Check** : The distance between the prediction and the centroid of historical training data is measured. 

2. **Cross-Agent Consistency** : If the RiskAgent reports 98 % confidence but the FraudAgent detects a structural anomaly in the same payload, the Decision Gate penalizes the overall confidence below the threshold. 

   - If the AI falsely reports high confidence, the system relies on the **Financial Determinism Principle (FDP)** as a last resort: any signal that violates a hard-coded invariant (e.g., **RI-01: Balance < 0** ) is rejected regardless of the AI's score.

## **THREAT MODEL FOR AI-ASSISTED FINANCIAL SYSTEMS** 

The integration of artificial intelligence into financial infrastructure introduces new categories of operational and security risks. Unlike deterministic software systems, machine learning models operate using probabilistic inference, which may produce unpredictable outputs under anomalous or adversarial conditions. 

This paper considers the following primary threat vectors: 

### **AI EXECUTION RISK** 

If AI systems are allowed to directly execute financial transactions, model errors, hallucinations, or adversarial inputs could trigger unauthorized ledger mutations. 

### **MODEL DRIFT** 

Model drift is a documented systemic risk; AI agents operating in volatile markets have been shown to degrade their accuracy by up to 15 % per month if they lack active drift monitoring mechanisms **[8]** . Ares-Nexus addresses this challenge with the **ModelDriftDetector** , which acts as a safety **'circuit breaker** ' if the AI variance exceeds regulatory confidence thresholds. Machine learning models may gradually diverge from their original training distribution, producing increasingly unreliable recommendations. 

### **ADVERSARIAL INPUT MANIPULATION** 

Attackers may craft input data designed to manipulate model inference results. 

### **REGULATORY NON-DETERMINISM** 

Probabilistic systems may produce inconsistent outputs for identical inputs, violating regulatory expectations for deterministic processing. 

### **DATA PRIVACY LEAKAGE** 

AI systems operating on sensitive financial data may unintentionally expose confidential information through model inference or training processes. 

### **MITIGATION VIA NEAI ARCHITECTURE** 

The Non-Executive AI architecture mitigates these risks by ensuring that artificial intelligence components operate exclusively as advisory systems. 

All AI outputs must pass through deterministic policy evaluation gates before any financial state transition can be executed. As a result, probabilistic inference cannot directly mutate the financial ledger.

### **BASEL IV CAPITAL EFFICIENCY MODEL** 

Modern banking regulation under the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision requires financial institutions to maintain capital buffers proportional to their Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA). Operational inefficiencies in financial reconciliation and settlement pipelines can artificially inflate capital requirements by increasing exposure uncertainty during reconciliation windows. 

This effect is modeled as the **Reconciliation Exposure Inflation Factor (REIF)** : 

#### **_REIF = Unreconciled Exposure / Total Exposure_** 

#### Where: 

- **Unreconciled Exposure** represents financial positions whose state has not yet been deterministically reconciled within the settlement system. 

- **Total Exposure** represents the total exposure under regulatory reporting. 

In legacy financial systems with asynchronous reconciliation pipelines, reconciliation windows may extend from several minutes to multiple hours, particularly in high-volume trading environments. This delay increases temporary exposure uncertainty, which regulators may interpret conservatively when computing capital buffers. 

By enforcing deterministic event-sourced settlement with millisecond reconciliation guarantees, the Ares-Nexus architecture reduces reconciliation windows to near-real-time execution intervals. This architectural property reduces the magnitude and duration of unreconciled exposure states, thereby minimizing regulatory capital inflation derived from operational latency. 

While precise capital impact varies across regulatory jurisdictions and asset classes, the deterministic settlement guarantees of Ares-Nexus provide a structural mechanism for reducing operationally induced RWA inflation.

### **LIMITATIONS** 

Despite the architectural advantages of the Ares-Nexus model which provides strong guarantees regarding deterministic financial execution and AI isolation, several limitations must be acknowledged when considering its deployment in real-world financial institutions. 

Using **snapshots** (every 100 events) is vital for maintaining a 28 seconds MTTR, but it introduces a nuance to auditing. While the current state is instantly rebuilt from the last snapshot, the **Full “TimeTravel” Audit Window** requires access to the historical log (Cold Storage). 

- **Strategy:** Ares-Nexus keeps events in Hot Storage (PostgreSQL) for 90 days for real-time auditing. 

- **Limitation** : Rebuilding state queries from more than one year ago require hydrating data from **Azure Blob Storage** , which increases audit latency from milliseconds to minutes. 

##### **EVENT LOG GROWTH** 

Event sourcing architectures inherently produce continuously growing event logs. Over long operational periods, financial institutions may accumulate billions of events. 

To mitigate this, periodic snapshotting strategies must be implemented to maintain efficient aggregate reconstruction times. 

_HISTORICAL RECOVERY COST (HRC) & SLA METRIC_ 

To balance operational efficiency with auditing requirements, Ares-Nexus defines **Historical Recovery Cost (HRC)** as the cumulative latency required to reconstruct a financial statement from events in cold storage. 

- Hot Storage (0-90 days): Reconstruction SLA of < 500 ms (PostgreSQL/Marten). 

- Cold Storage (> 1 year): Reconstruction SLA of ~15-20 minutes per million events. This is a conscious design decision: we prioritize a 28-second MTTR for critical operational failures, accepting asynchronous latency for deep historical audits. This optimizes Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) by 60% by not maintaining petabytes of historical logs on highperformance disks (SSD/Premium Storage). 

##### **PROJECTION CONSISTENCY** 

Read models in event-sourced systems are typically eventually consistent. Although financial ledger state remains deterministic, projections used for reporting or analytics may temporarily lag behind the authoritative event stream. 

##### **REGULATORY INTEGRATION COMPLEXITY** 

Financial institutions operating under strict regulatory oversight may require additional governance layers before adopting architectures that incorporate AI advisory systems. 

Approval processes for AI-assisted decision workflows may require validation from model risk management committees and regulatory supervisors. 

##### **OPERATIONAL COMPLEXITY** 

Event sourcing architectures introduce additional operational complexity compared to traditional CRUD-based systems. 

Engineering teams must maintain infrastructure for event storage, projection rebuilding, and replay capabilities, which may require specialized operational expertise. 

## **FAILURE SCENARIO, FUTURE WORK, CONCLUSION & REFERENCES** 

### **FAILURE SCENARIO ANALYSIS** 

Distributed financial systems must remain resilient under both infrastructure failures and component-level faults. Several failure scenarios were considered in the design of the Ares-Nexus architecture. 

##### **AI ADVISORY AGENT FAILURE** 

If an AI advisory agent becomes unavailable or produces invalid outputs, the deterministic settlement core remains unaffected. AI agents operate asynchronously and cannot directly execute financial transactions. 

##### **EVENT LOG CORRUPTION** 

Event sourcing architectures rely heavily on the integrity of the event log. To mitigate corruption risks, event streams are replicated across storage nodes and verified through checksum validation. 

##### **REPLAY MISMATCH** 

Deterministic replay validation ensures that ledger state reconstructed from event streams always matches the authoritative system state. Any mismatch triggers reconciliation procedures. 

##### **MESSAGE DUPLICATION** 

These mechanisms collectively ensure that failure conditions cannot compromise deterministic financial execution. 

##### **FAILURE SCENARIO: THE "FALSE POSITIVE FRAUD"** 

**Scenario** : The **_RiskAgent_** (AI) identifies an RMBS payment pattern as "suspicious of fraud" based on a flawed probabilistic correlation and issues a **REJECT** signal. 

1. **AI Signal:** **_RiskAgent_** → **REJECT** (96 % Confidence). 

2. **Interception:** The Decision Gate receives the signal. 

3. **Invariant Evaluation:** The Gate checks invariant RI-03 (Settlement Sum) and consults the Deterministic Whitelist of Tier-1 counterparties. 

4. **Conflict Resolution:** Since the transaction complies with all hard-coded legal requirements and the risk detected by the AI is purely heuristic, the Gate applies the **FDP** (False Positive Dispute Resolution). 

5. **Result:** The Gate overrides the AI recommendation, approves the command, and records in the **Audit Log:** " _AI Advisory Rejected: Heuristic False Positive. Deterministic Rules Prevailed_ ." 

6. **Value:** An unjustified liquidity block is avoided, closing the "Trust Gap”. 

While traditional settlement systems would have unnecessarily locked up liquidity based on a faulty risk signal, Ares-Nexus, by applying the **Financial Determinism Principle (FDP)** , guarantees uninterrupted operation. This transforms the infrastructure of a 'capital-locking cost center' into a resilience engine that protects cash flow, combining technical accuracy with an unbeatable business benefit: the elimination of the opportunity cost of AI false positives.
